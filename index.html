Tokenized lab data.

1Scope

Each result becomes a sealed record: a SHA-256 fingerprint of five of its recorded fields, signed and linked to the record before it, so a later change shows. Anchoring batches on a public network is an upcoming feature; only a fingerprint will go on the network, never the values.

1.1Regulatory position

Built toward the audit-trail expectations of 21 CFR Part 11, EU Annex 11 and ISO/IEC 17025:2017. Not validated. No electronic signatures yet. Not a substitute for your computerized-system validation or SOPs.

2Record history

The whole synthetic sequence, as an auditor would page through it. When the table comes into view, your browser checks every record once and marks each result; after that nothing moves.

3Today, upcoming and not claimed

  • Alpha console · demo in your browserRuns on a synthetic lab, only in your browser. Each record gets a SHA-256 fingerprint, a link to the record before it (the first record links to a fixed start value of 64 zeros), and a signature from a demo key made in your browser. Corrections are new records with a reason, and every check is recomputed after each change. Its anchor is simulated, with no network call.
  • These pagesThe same construction on one synthetic sequence, signed with a demo key at build. Your browser recomputes every check.
  • Alpha app · not publicImports HPLC CSV exports. Each peak area becomes a record of instrument, measurement type, value, unit and capture time, fingerprinted, linked and signed by one server key. The capture time is an injection time typed at import, or the server’s clock if none is given.
  • UpcomingAnchoring batch fingerprints on a public test network, a shared registry, reviewer sign-off, server-side verification in the app, per-analyst signatures, sample and method metadata in the seal, privacy on public networks, agentic workflows and Filecoin integration. Every anchor on this site and in the console is simulated.
  • Not claimedNo customers, no instrument capture, no electronic signatures and no validation. Nothing on this site or in the console writes to a public network.

BasisThe same five fields and link rule in the alpha app’s HPLC import and on these pages; in the app, the instrument field is its internal instrument number. The console documents its own payload in its Method panel.

4Where it fits

Your instrument, CDS, LIMS and QA review stay as they are. In the alpha, Immutable QC sits beside the CDS export. The alpha app fingerprints, links and signs each imported peak area with one server key; checking them in the app is upcoming. On these pages and in the console the record history is tamper-evident, so a later change to a sealed value fails its checks. Changes made before import are not covered.

5What would go public Upcoming

Stays with the lab

  • Peak areas and every other value
  • Sample, method and sequence records
  • Names, reasons and review notes
  • The record history itself

Would go on a public network

Nothing on this site writes to a network. Once anchoring becomes available, one batch fingerprint will go on it for a whole run of records. It is a 64-character code; here it is the link of record 8, which depends on records 1 to 8.

0a35688a4c3ec82a5e621cede1f173a6d436ade0e20f159dca92c2a3711e3dc1

Simulated here, with no network call. Anyone who holds the records can recompute it and see whether they still match.

A fingerprint is not encryption. How a record is sealed, section 9, explains why, and what is upcoming.

6Agentic workflows Upcoming

In an intelligence explosion, human review can’t keep up with every result; records have to verify themselves.

José A. Fernández Abreu, founder

An agent would run the same four checks as Check a record before it uses a result. Passing them says a result has not changed since it was sealed; it does not say the result was right when it was captured.

Check a record shows one record as an agent would receive it. There is no API yet.
