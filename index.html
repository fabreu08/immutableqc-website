The Problem

Laboratory data is vulnerable at every step

Manual transcription errors

Data moves from instrument to LIMS via manual entry or fragile CSV exports. Errors propagate. The chain of custody breaks before it begins.

Undetectable tampering

Without cryptographic verification, altered records are indistinguishable from authentic ones. Auditors must trust process, not math.

Compliance overhead

21 CFR Part 11 and ALCOA+ demand complete, consistent, enduring records. Traditional systems generate paper trails, not proofs.

FDA Warning Letters from 2023 to 2024 show data integrity violations as a leading cause of regulatory action against analytical laboratories. Industry context — not a product claim.

The Solution

Cryptographic signing at the source

IQC sits between your instruments and your data systems. It captures measurements the moment they are generated — before human hands touch them — and commits them to a tamper-evident audit trail.

Instrument-grade provenance

Every measurement is signed at capture. Signature, timestamp, and instrument metadata form the chain of custody.

Zero manual transcription

Direct instrument integration. No CSV copy-paste. No opportunity for post-hoc manipulation.

Mathematical audit trails

Regulators verify integrity via cryptographic proof. Evidence that data has not changed since capture.

How It Works

From instrument to immutable record

  1. 01

    Connect

    HPLC, mass spectrometers, pH meters, and sensors via RS-232, TCP/IP, or file watchers.

  2. 02

    Capture

    Raw measurement data is intercepted at generation. Calibration metadata is captured with it.

  3. 03

    Sign & commit

    Each measurement is signed and appended to a local hash-chained ledger. Periodic roots go to a public registry.

  4. 04

    Verify

    Auditors confirm data has not been altered since capture — without exposing proprietary methods or raw results.

Technical implementation

Built on proven cryptographic standards

IQC adapts attestation patterns from supply-chain security and distributed systems research for laboratory compliance. Production signing target is ECDSA secp256k1.

Sigstore

Certificate transparency and keyless signing patterns for short-lived keys and transparent logs.

OpenTimestamps

Distributed timestamping for proof of existence without bloating a registry.

Alignment with automated result verification and exception handling in clinical labs.
