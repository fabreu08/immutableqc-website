How a record is sealed

1Scope

How the alpha turns one result into a sealed record, step by step, with record {{sn}} of the synthetic sequence {{seq}} as the example. Every value on this page was computed and checked when the page was built.

2The sealed fields

Five fields, joined with a vertical bar in this order: instrument, measurement type, value, unit and capture time. For record {{sn}}, a peak area in counts ({{pbytes}} bytes, UTF-8):

{{payload}}

BasisThis is the payload the alpha app’s HPLC import seals for each peak area.

2.1What is not sealed yet

In this demo, injection, sample id and retention time are shown beside the record as context. The alpha app keeps retention time unsealed and does not keep sample or method yet. Sealing sample and method metadata inside the record is on the roadmap.

3The fingerprint

r{{sn}} = SHA-256 of the payload: {{rShort}}. Change one character of any field and the fingerprint changes completely.

BasisSHA-256, FIPS 180-4.

4The link to the previous record

hn = SHA-256 of hn−1, rn and n, written one after another as text, starting from 64 zeros. Record {{sn}}’s link is {{hShort}}. A change to any sealed field breaks that record’s link and every later one.

5The signature (demo)

Each fingerprint is signed with ECDSA P-256 by a demo key whose private half was discarded after the build. Its public key ships with the pages that check it, which shows the method but is not independent evidence: in use, the key would be published outside the lab’s system. {{demoSig}}

In the record checker, an edited record’s signature still fails after the history is rewritten, but only because the demo key was discarded. In the alpha, whoever holds the one server key could re-sign an edited record; only a fingerprint anchored outside the lab beforehand would still disagree.

A digital signature on a record’s bytes is not an electronic signature by a person under 21 CFR Part 11.

BasisECDSA, FIPS 186-5.

6Anchoring a batch

On this site the batch fingerprint is the last link of the batch, and the anchor is simulated, with no network call. The alpha console builds its batch fingerprint differently, from every three records, and lets you write it by hand to a registry you deploy yourself on a public test network; no shared registry exists yet. Only fingerprints go on the network; the values stay with the lab.

7Corrections

A correction is a new record with a reason, appended after the newest one. The original stays as sealed and keeps verifying; the new head is not anchored until the next batch.
