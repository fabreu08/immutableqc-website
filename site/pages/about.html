The demo data is synthetic. Nothing on this site is validated for regulated use.

4Why

Undocumented work did not happen. Labs already keep audit trails; the aim is a record that someone outside the lab can check from the record itself: a reviewer, an auditor, or later an AI agent.

5Questions

5.1Do I need to buy or hold anything?

No. There is nothing to buy, hold or connect. These pages and the alpha console run in your browser, with no sign-up.

5.2Is my data put on a blockchain?

No. Your values stay with the lab. The most that goes on a public test network, which is a blockchain run for testing, is one batch fingerprint: 64 hex characters for a whole run of records. In the alpha that step is manual, and on this site and in the console it is simulated. Because a plain fingerprint of a few guessable fields could in principle be matched by trying likely values, salted commitments and later zero-knowledge proofs are on the roadmap.

5.3Does it replace my CDS, LIMS or audit trail?

No. It sits beside the CDS export and keeps a tamper-evident record history of the results imported from it. Your CDS audit trail, LIMS, SOPs and validation stay as they are.

5.4Can I use it for GMP records today?

Not yet. It is an open alpha on synthetic data: not validated, with no electronic signatures, no user accounts and no instrument capture. Use it to judge the approach, and keep regulated records in your validated systems.

5.5What does a fingerprint cover?

In the alpha, five fields: instrument, measurement type, value, unit and capture time, joined by a vertical bar and hashed with SHA-256. Sample, injection and retention time are shown for context only; sealing sample and method metadata is on the roadmap.

5.6What if someone edits the database directly?

The edited record no longer matches its fingerprint or its signature, and the link into the next record breaks. If they also rewrite every later link, the links look consistent again; the signature still fails, and the replayed history no longer matches a fingerprint anchored outside the lab beforehand. In the alpha one server key signs, so whoever holds that key could re-sign an edit, and anchoring is a manual step.
