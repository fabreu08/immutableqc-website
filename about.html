4Why it exists

In an automated lab, results arrive faster than anyone can review them, and models train on them and choose the next experiment. In a regulated lab, work that is not documented is treated as not done. Both already keep records; the aim is a record that someone outside the system can check from the record itself: a reviewer, an auditor, a model, or an AI agent.

5Questions

5.1What kinds of labs is this for?

Automated research labs first: biotechnology, microbiology, chemistry and materials science, where self-driving labs, robotic workcells, cloud labs, AI-first research companies and CROs that hand automated data to clients produce results faster than people can review them, and models train on them. The founder’s own background is materials science, chemistry and microbiology. Regulated QC labs are the expansion path, not the opening; the Regulatory map is written for them.

5.2Do I need to buy or hold anything?

No. There is nothing to buy, hold or connect. These pages and the alpha console run in your browser, with no sign-up.

5.3Is my data put on a blockchain?

No. Nothing on this site or in the alpha console writes to any network, and the values you enter stay in your browser. Anchoring is an upcoming feature. By default it will put only one batch fingerprint, a 64-character code for a whole run of records, on a public test network (a blockchain run for testing). A lab that chooses the encrypted archive, also upcoming, would store its records on Filecoin too, encrypted in the lab first and never readable there. On this site and in the console the anchor is simulated.

5.4Does it replace my orchestrator, ELN, CDS, LIMS or audit trail?

No. Today it sits beside an HPLC CSV export; upcoming, it would sit in or beside the orchestrator and hand each sealed result on unchanged. The alpha app fingerprints, links and signs each imported peak area with one server key; checking them in the app is upcoming. Your orchestrator, CDS audit trail, ELN, LIMS, SOPs and validation stay as they are. In the alpha the fingerprint covers five fields: instrument, measurement type, value, unit and capture time, joined by a vertical bar and hashed with SHA-256; sample, injection and retention time are shown for context only, and sealing sample and method metadata is upcoming.

5.5Can I use it for GMP records today?

No. It is an open alpha on synthetic data: not validated, with no electronic signatures, no user accounts and no capture at the instrument or orchestrator. Use it to judge the approach, and keep regulated records in your validated systems.

5.6What if someone edits the database directly?

The edited record no longer matches its fingerprint, its signature fails, and the link into the next record breaks. Someone who also rewrites every later link and re-signs with the signing key can make the history consistent again. Deleting a record and relinking the ones after it needs no key at all, because each signature covers only its own record’s fingerprint. In the alpha one server key signs, so whoever holds it could do either. Only a fingerprint anchored outside the lab beforehand would still show it, and anchoring is upcoming.

5.7What about sabotage and espionage?

Most bad data is not malicious: a script, a sync conflict, a crash, a restored backup, a migration or a model that filled in a blank. Sabotage is the minority case, and the same checks cover it, within limits.

Detected today, wherever the checks are run (these pages, the console, or the recipe in How a record is sealed; checking in the alpha app is upcoming): a sealed value changed after sealing by anyone without the signing key, whoever it was and why; the fingerprint, the signature and the link into the next record fail. A history rewritten with the key would show only against an anchor taken earlier. A record deleted, moved or copied, with the rest relinked, needs no key today; it would show against an earlier anchor too, and through signatures that cover each record’s position when done by anyone without the key. Both are upcoming.

Not detected: anything before sealing, such as a swapped sample, a miscalibrated instrument or compromised robot software. In the alpha app, records are sealed at import; sealing at capture (upcoming) would narrow that window, not close it.

Espionage is not detected: a seal makes a change show, not a copy. What limits it is that nothing readable leaves the lab. Anchoring would put only a fingerprint on a public network, and once salted (upcoming) it would reveal no value; values would leave only encrypted in the lab, with keys that stay there, and only if the lab chooses the encrypted archive (upcoming). Protecting the records, the keys and the people who hold them stays with the lab; Immutable QC would be one part of that, not a security program.
